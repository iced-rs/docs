1//! Snapping coordinates to the physical pixel grid.
2//!
3//! # Why the nudge
4//!
5//! Snapping exists so that primitive edges land on the pixel grid and
6//! render as crisp, fully opaque pixels instead of anti-aliased seams (see
7//! [`CRISP`]). But `round`'s decision boundary — the half-pixel — is not
8//! stable under floating-point arithmetic: a coordinate meant to land
9//! exactly on a half-pixel (e.g. `5.25` logical pixels at 2× scale,
10//! `10.5` physical) can be computed a hair below it (e.g. `10.4998`),
11//! where plain `round` rounds down. The edge is then off the grid — the
12//! anti-aliased seam that snapping exists to remove — and one pixel away
13//! from its neighbours, which computed the same boundary cleanly.
14//!
15//! And half-pixels are common in UIs, not a corner case: centering and
16//! equal space distribution are constant in layouts, and `.25` logical
17//! pixels land exactly on physical half-pixels at 2× scale.
18//!
19//! Nudging each edge by [`NUDGE`] before rounding pulls the boundary down
20//! to `.499`: any coordinate within `0.001` below a half-pixel now rounds
21//! up onto the grid, so floating-point noise can no longer split a
22//! half-pixel edge off the grid. Coordinates further from the boundary are
23//! unaffected.
24//!
25//! # Consistency
26//!
27//! The same convention — nudging by [`NUDGE`] before rounding — is applied
28//! by the `quad` vertex shader (see `shader/quad/snap.wgsl`) and by every
29//! CPU-side snapping of these coordinates (the clip (scissor) bounds of the
30//! quad, triangle, image and text passes). The two must never diverge, or
31//! clip bounds may end up one pixel off from the snapped edges they clip.
32//!
33//! [`CRISP`]: crate::core::renderer::CRISP
34use crate::core::{Rectangle, Vector};
35
36/// The nudge applied when snapping a coordinate to the pixel grid.
37///
38/// Coordinates landing just below a half-pixel boundary round up because
39/// of it.
40///
41/// This must stay in sync with `nudge` in
42/// `shader/quad/snap.wgsl`.
43pub const NUDGE: f32 = 0.001;
44
45/// Snaps `bounds` to the physical pixel grid.
46///
47/// It uses the same rounding convention as the `quad` vertex shader
48/// (see `shader/quad/snap.wgsl`): each edge is nudged by [`NUDGE`] before
49/// being rounded, so that coordinates landing just below a half-pixel
50/// boundary round up.
51///
52/// This ensures that clip (scissor) bounds align exactly with the snapped
53/// edges of the [`Quad`] primitives drawn inside them.
54///
55/// Sub-pixel results are rejected, by [`Rectangle::snap`] itself.
56///
57/// [`Quad`]: crate::core::renderer::Quad
58pub fn snap(bounds: Rectangle) -> Option<Rectangle<u32>> {
59    (bounds + Vector::new(NUDGE, NUDGE)).snap()
60}
